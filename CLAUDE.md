# Expo Planner

A browser app for planning crew for exhibition build-up (montering) and tear-down (demontering). It replaces the planner's Excel workbook (`Bemanning_Behov_24 måneder.xlsx`), which it does not read: the app stands on the source exports alone. The workbook was the model for the rules and the test data while the app was built.

Open work is listed in [docs/TODO.md](docs/TODO.md). How the old workbook works is described in [docs/kalender-workbook.md](docs/kalender-workbook.md). The files in `design_docs/` come from an abandoned earlier attempt; read them for background only, they are not requirements.

## Commands

```bash
npm run dev     # dev server
npm test        # Vitest, all tests
npm run build   # type check (tsc -b) + production build
npm run lint    # oxlint
```

Run `npx tsc -b` after edits; tests are type-checked through `tsconfig.test.json`, not by Vitest.

## What the app does

Eight tabs, all in Norwegian:

- **Kalender** – the planning workspace. Date header, hall calendar and staffing totals are pinned above the planning rows, and right above the rows sits the planning bar with the tools (`kalender/PlanBar.tsx`): Velg, Fordel behov and Tøm (keys V, F, T; Shift-drag and Alt-drag give the pencil and the eraser for one stroke), filter, grouping and zoom. «Steder» in the page header (`kalender/AreaMenu.tsx`) is a tree of the planner's areas with their halls, to tick the ones to work with, and under it the statuses of Venyou whose bookings count, in both modes: the hall calendar shows the ticked halls and the bookings of the ticked statuses, a project booked in unticked halls or with unticked statuses alone is left out of the rows and of Bemanning's projects, and its FTE out of the need and the demand. The hall calendar has a compact view, as the project lines of Bemanning have (`DensityToggle`): a thin bar per phase without letters or names. What is unticked is kept per browser (`HallFilter` in `domain/areas.ts`). A right-click on a planning cell opens a menu for the cell and its row, and the panel toggle in the page header slides row details in over the grid (`kalender/RowInspector.tsx`). The Avvik line is a heat map (`kalender/heat.ts`), switched off under Innstillinger. The rows form a hierarchy the planner arranges («Grupper etter»: Prosjekt, Arbeidsfase, Hall/Sted, Kompetanse, Avd., in any order); FTE per day is stored on the rows and every level above sums. A level can be switched from Σ to entry (✎): a number typed there is shared out over its rows by required hours (`domain/spread.ts`). The days in a row a planning row has FTE are a span, and its first and last cell are also its grips, with any tool: a press there dragged sideways pulls that end along the days (`stretchSpan` in `domain/spread.ts`, `pullPreview` in `kalender/strokes.ts`), while a click still selects the cell and a drag up or down selects or draws as from any cell. Outward the new working days get the span's crew, inward the days let go of are cleared and the day at the other end stays; an end stops on the day before the row's next planned day. Shift held while it is pulled stops the end where the row's demand is met, Alt keeps the sum and shares it again; held at the press they mean what they do on any cell. The cells, the Avvik line and the row's Planlagt and Δ show what the pull would leave while it lasts. What a stroke would do to the rows is drawn one way, for the pencil, the eraser and a pull alike, as a stroke is in Bemanning: the days that would get FTE as a tint of the row's phase colour with their figures, the days that would lose it hatched in that colour with their figures kept, and what the stroke does on one pill over the days and lines it changes (`strokePill` in `kalender/strokes.ts`, drawn by `kalender/StrokeBox.tsx`, which Bemanning draws its strokes and its selection with too; in the plan the pill is ink, and the blocking alert's chip where the stroke only takes FTE away). A level in entry has no spans and keeps the figures in the accent and the eraser's red. A press selects nothing by itself: a cell is selected when the mouse is let go on it, a range once the drag reaches another cell, and while a stroke is drawn or an end is pulled no cell is marked as selected; a pull leaves what was selected as it was. The cell in focus has a thin ring and no tint, and the tint is for the rest of a range. The pencil («Fordel behov») shares what is left of a line's demand over the working days drawn across. Each row shows its window, the build-up or tear-down days of its project in its hall (`domain/windows.ts`), and ✦ («Foreslå plan») fills rows over their windows. A click on a project's name brings its days into view, in narrower columns if they do not fit (`fitSpan` in `kalender/layout.ts`), and keeps its halls and bars lit in the hall calendar until it is clicked again or Escape is pressed. The line of a top level stays at the top of the rows while the lines under it scroll past (`levelSections` in `kalender/rows.ts`), and a click on an event's bar in the hall calendar scrolls the rows to its project.
  The Kalender has two modes, switched first in the planning bar: **Plan**, the rows above, and **Bemanning**, who does the work: the permanent staff by name against the hours that remain per competence and day, over the whole period. In Bemanning the date header stays, the hall calendar becomes one line per project in view (`bemanning/ProjectLines.tsx`, `projectsInView.ts`), the staffing lines become the demand per competence, read from the planned FTE (`DemandRows.tsx`), which counts live while a stroke is drawn over the people: the days it covers and the «t igjen» of the label show what would remain after it (`shownBalance` in `BemanningScope.tsx`), and the rows become the people (`PeopleRows.tsx`). The switch zooms between the two column widths (`kalender/zoom.ts`, `useModeZoom.ts`). The width of a day is the style variable `--col-w`; lay out by it (`daysWide` in `kalender/layout.ts`), never by a width in pixels from `colW`, or the zoom has to draw every line again on each frame. A competence in focus is also the brush: it fills free time only, whole days or half, one person's hours open as a track of time per day with overtime (`UnfoldedPerson.tsx`, `TimeTrack.tsx`), and sickness gives a person's hours back to the demand. The days in a row a person has one competence are drawn as one run with one name («Vegger · 5 d», `runs.ts`): a drawing only, the blocks are stored and edited per day as before, and another competence or an empty day ends a run, so a weekend does unless it is worked. The brush skips weekends and holidays when it is drawn across working days, and fills one from the start to the end of the normal day when it is picked by itself (`onlyOffDays`); such a day is drawn as a working day's, without hatch. Hatching on a person's day means absence: a neutral block where nothing is planned, and the planned block itself hatched in its colour where the absence cuts into it, with one badge for the reason. Absence for a part of a day takes only the time it covers out of a block (`workedParts`): those hours are back in the demand, the rest of the block counts, and only that stretch is hatched. A hatch says hours are not there: a person who is not available, hours of a block that do not count, and the hours the eraser would take. The rest of what a stroke would do is drawn flat: a tint where the brush would put time, a fainter tint on the day a move comes from, no edge on the blocks under the eraser, and one thin outline with a pill that sits on it. With any tool, an end of a run or of a single day is pulled along the days (`stretchRun` in `runs.ts`): outward the days get what the brush would give them drawn across, inward the run's blocks on them are removed, the day at the other end always stays, and the stroke keeps to that person's line. The width of a day is its normal day there, and the end stops where every two hours of it are worked (`stretchStops`: 09:00, 11:00, 13:30 and the end of the day with lunch from 11:30; counted in paid hours, so they hold wherever the lunch is laid): the last day is filled no further than the stop, and pulled inward the block the end lands in is cut to it. A brush stroke that is drawn out stops the same way on the day under the pointer, while a click fills its day. In columns too narrow to aim at two hours, both go by whole days. With Shift held while either is drawn out, the stroke goes no further than where the demand is met (`cellsToDemand` in `staffing.ts`): it ends on the last of its days that lacks hours of the competence, filled to the quarter of an hour they ask; Shift at the press of the brush is half days, as before. The lunch break is the planner's to lay, under Innstillinger → «Bemanning og normaltid» (`Settings.workday`). With Velg, too, a day's blocks are dragged to another day or person, and selected days are copied and pasted: a paste lands on the lines from the one under the pointer, the first person copied on that line and the next on the line under it. A click on a name opens the Person panel (`PersonPanel.tsx`): overtime per week against a limit, and absence entered as periods. What the parts of the mode share is held by `BemanningScope.tsx`, so the grid is not drawn again when the pointer moves over the people. Designed in `design_docs/bemanning-v2/`, on the rules of `design_docs/bemanning/RULES.md`.
- **Behov** – the demand ledger per project: Visma lines, the planner's own lines and earlier years. "I plan" takes a Visma line into the demand that is planned with («Planlagt»).
- **VenYou** (the hall ledger, `ui/haller/`) – every hall booking from Venyou, with a tick for whether it shows in the Kalender. The project number of an event is shown, not typed: it is read from Prosjekter.
- **Prosjekter** – the projects: a number each, and every name a project goes by in the sources, a row per project. The number is the id that demand, orders and planning rows point at. An event in Venyou gets the number of the project that carries its name in its year. Events without a project are listed first, each with what it is offered: the project of its year whose name is like its own and whose days do not speak against it (`nameLikeness`, `nameWithin`, `ProjectRow.match`), else the number of its year after the project it was another year («27231» after «26231», `ProjectRow.series`, only where that number is free), else a number of its own (the year and three letters, «26VAM»). One click takes the offer, or the number of the project it belongs to is typed. A number that comes with orders and is like a project under another number can take over from it (`withNumberTakingOver`). A project given another number takes its planning rows and the planner's own demand lines along.
- **Hallregler** (`ui/behov/HallRules.tsx`) – the rules that place a Hall/Sted text of the demand in a hall, all of them the planner's own: places that stand for several halls (named, renamed, and their halls ticked in a list), words that mean a place, in the order they are tried, and the choices made for single texts. The page also holds the areas («Områder»: a name and its halls, as «NV HALLS»), which place nothing: they are what «Steder» in the Kalender lists the halls by. One part is shown at a time, picked in the bar of the page (`Part` in `HallRules.tsx`), so no table is scrolled past to reach another: the texts that lack a hall, the places, the rules for words and the choices for single texts, which are last since their list grows long, and apart from them the areas. The first part is the texts of the demand that no rule places (`Unplaced.tsx`), a row per text with its lines and hours, to place there with one choice or by the offer it has. The panel that tries a text and shows the order the rules are tried in, with the lines each step places (`PlacingPanel.tsx`), slides in over the right edge from the toggle in the bar or a click on a text, as row details do in the Kalender. Reached from Behov, where «Regel» says which of them placed a line.
- **KPI** – how each Visma product type is read, and its rates: the unit in use, the competence, and units per person-hour for montering and demontering, a row per product type and unit. Product types found in the Visma exports are listed before they are set up, and «Mangler» says what each still lacks.
- **Personell** – the permanent staff with their competences. No two people have the same name: a file tells them apart by it.
- **Kompetanser** – the name of each competence, how it is shown (short name, colour, order), and what names it. A competence has one name: changed here, it is changed on the product types, the demand, the planning rows, the people and their blocks (`renameCompetence` in `domain/competences.ts`).

Prosjekter, Hallregler, KPI, Personell and Kompetanser are the planner's own tables, and each has «Eksporter» and «Importer fra fil» (`ui/TableFile.tsx`, with the reading and the wait for merge or replace in `ui/useTableFile.ts`): the table as an .xlsx file to keep or edit in Excel, read back with merge or replace.

Sources read from files: the Venyou export (`location_format_from-…_to-….xlsx`), Visma exports (`utskrift_visma_….xlsx`), and optionally `Prosjekt.xlsx` (through «Importer fra fil» on Prosjekter) and `Kpier.xlsx` as one-time shortcuts. KPI reads and writes its own file (`import/kpiFile.ts`): «Eksporter» writes the table as .xlsx, and «Importer fra fil» reads that file, or `Kpier.xlsx`, with merge or replace. The Nøkkeltall workbook is not read (2026-10-09); which unit is in use for a product type with several is chosen in the app, or marked «I bruk» in the file.

## How it fits together

- `src/domain/` – pure logic, no React and no storage. Start here.
  - `types.ts` – the `Workspace` and its records.
  - `visma.ts` – Visma booking lines → demand lines. One line per project × Avdeling × work type × hall; hours = quantity ÷ rate × (1 − Effekt). Units `ordre` and `stands` count stands; other units sum `Totalt antall`.
  - `kpi.ts` – the KPI table: product types with their unit in use, competence and rates, what each lacks, and merge/replace for imports.
  - `venue.ts`, `venueImport.ts` – the hall calendar, merging a Venyou export, hidden bookings.
  - `projects.ts` – the project table: a row per name a project goes by (`ProjectRef`: name, number, and a year only where the number does not begin with it). Matching an event to a project by name and year, the suggested numbers, the edits of the table, what follows a number (`projectFollowers`), and the rows of the Prosjekter tab (`projectRows`).
  - `locations.ts` – places a demand line in a hall of the hall ledger, for its project, and says what placed it (`placeOf`, shown as «Regel» on Behov): the planner's choice for that Hall/Sted text in the project, else the choice for the text in every project, else the hall or place the whole text names, however it is spelled («Studio 3» is STUDIO3; `spelled`), else the first of the planner's word rules whose words it holds, else «Mangler hall». Places that stand for several halls («B» for B1 to B4, with their days together in the Kalender) and the word rules («a text that holds scene counts under C») and the choices for texts are the planner's own, one record (`HallRules`, stored as `hallRules`: `places`, `phrases`, `choices`). What is stored is all there is: nothing is added while a text is read, and nothing about which halls belong together is in the code. A place collects its halls unless the planner unticks it (`collectedIn`): what lands in «B1» counts under «B», and the halls of such a place are no places of their own. The first time halls are read in from Venyou, examples are written into the rules (`exampleRules`, in `importVenue`): the letters several numbered halls share as places, and a letter with one numbered hall as a choice («A» is «A1», which places «Hall A»), to change or delete. They are never added again. The code itself reads only names: the whole text is the name of a hall or a place. «Ufordelt» (`PROJECT_HALLS`) is the place of demand that is not shared out on halls, ordered as one sum for several, as «Hall C, D, E»; it has the days of all the project's halls. It holds that demand alone, where a planning row for «Alle haller» covers the demand of every place. It is given by the planner or a rule, never fallen to. A text left as «Mangler hall» is offered a place (`suggestHall`), in the Plassering cell and for all at once: the one place it names somewhere in it, in one word or several («cafe hall D», «Kafé ved Studio 3»), «Ufordelt» where it names several, or the only hall of its project where it names none, which is a choice for that project alone. The line keeps its own text.
  - `hallReview.ts` – the demand as the rules place it (`reviewPlacing`): the lines per step of the order, what each choice and each rule for words is used for, and the texts that are left, with their offers. Hallregler and the notice on Behov count from it.
  - `areas.ts` – the halls gathered in the planner's areas (`HallRules.areas`), as a tree, what the planner has unticked of it in the Kalender, and the projects that are out of sight with it. An area decides what is shown, never where demand counts; that is the places of `locations.ts`.
  - `plannedRows.ts` – demand under «Planlagt» shows as suggested Kalender rows, one per project × phase × hall × competence × Avd. A row with no hall or Avd. covers all of them.
  - `calc.ts` – required hours per row (like the workbook's TIMER column), daily need, capacity.
  - `calendarRange.ts` – the Kalender's period follows the hall bookings.
  - `staffing.ts` – the rules of Bemanning: paid hours and overtime, when a person can work, painting and editing assignments, and the balance of demand and assigned hours per competence and day. `competences.ts` – the list of competences and their styles, and edits to people.
- `src/import/` – file readers and writers. `xlsx.ts` is a small own reader (cached values only) and writer (`writeXlsx`: plain values under a heading); a table the app exports is written with it and read back through `findHeader`, as `kpiFile.ts`, `personellFile.ts`, `kompetanserFile.ts`, `prosjekterFile.ts` and `hallreglerFile.ts` (a sheet per list) do. What a file changes is worked out in the domain (`mergeKpi`, `mergePersons`, `mergeCompetenceStyles`, `mergeProjectList`, `mergeHallRules` and their `diff…`), not in the reader; the counting of a diff is `diffBy` in `domain/tableDiff.ts`.
- `src/store/` – `db.ts` (Dexie/IndexedDB), `workspaceStore.tsx` (all mutations, each persisted and recorded for undo), `history.ts` (undo steps), `backup.ts`, `prefs.ts` (view preferences per browser, in localStorage).
- `src/ui/` – one folder per tab, and `bemanning/` for the Kalender's Bemanning mode. `kalender/Kalender.tsx` is a custom virtualized grid; `kalender/rows.ts` builds the row hierarchy from the chosen grouping, `kalender/strokes.ts` works out what the pencil, «Foreslå plan», a pull of a span's end and a paste would write, and `kalender/GridRows.tsx` holds the lines of the grid as memoized components. They get the selection as plain values per line (`CellEdit`) and their handlers through `useStableActions`; keep it that way, or every line is drawn again on each scroll frame. What the tabs share is in `common.tsx` (undo buttons, message banner, merge-or-replace dialog, and `SidePanel` with `PanelToggle`: the frame of a panel that slides in over a page, which row details, the Person panel and the panel of Hallregler are written in), `files.ts`, `fields.tsx` and the table of the ledgers: `DataTable.tsx` draws it from a list of columns (`Column` in `useTable.ts`), each saying what its cell shows and what it is filtered and sorted by; `useTable.ts` holds the filters and the sorting, `ColumnHead.tsx` is the heading, and the rules are in `columnFilter.ts` and `columnSort.ts`. A new table of rows is a `DataTable`, not a `<table>` of its own; Haller (events with their halls under them) and the summary on Behov are not flat lists and are written out; Haller still takes its headings and filters from `useTable` and `ColumnHead`, over its bookings, and has no sorting.
- `src/styles/` – the style sheets, imported in this order by `main.tsx`: `tokens.css` (fonts, colours and text sizes, light and dark), `base.css` (controls, the shell, menus, dialogs), `tables.css` (the ledgers of the other tabs), `kalender.css`, `inspector.css` and `bemanning.css`. Each rule is written once; do not add a later rule that overrides an earlier one, change the rule.
  The look of the Kalender follows `design_docs/kalender-v9/DESIGN-RULES.md`, which is normative: read it before changing a line, a fill or a weekend in the grid. In short: a rule is there to be read along, so only the date header and the lines that are typed in (rows, a level in entry, the people) have rules between the days; a line of figures that is read across has a rule under it and none between the days (the staffing lines of Plan, and a competence's line in Bemanning when it is not compact), which is the planner's own addition to the rules; a level has one line above it, over the days alone where it is not a top level, so the label column has lines above top levels only, and the strong line means a top level and nothing else; sections are set apart by a heading on a neutral tint, which is not a line of days: no weekends, no red days and no today line go through it (the planner's choice, where the rules have the weekends go on through); the planned days of a row are a span in the colour of its work phase, a bar behind the figures that is rounded where a run of days with FTE starts and ends, and the cell has no fill of its own (`run-start` and `run-end` in `AllocRow`); a folded level shows the span of the rows under it the same way, with its sums on it, in the colour of the phase most of the day's FTE is for (`stripOn` in `GroupRow`), and an open level shows none; a weekend is one step darker than its line and never see-through; a line (`.grid-row`) is never given a see-through border. The names differ from the mockup's: the four lines are `--grid-col`, `--grid-row`, `--grid-phase` and `--grid-top`, the weekends `--weekend` and `--band-we`, the tint of a section `--section`. The colour of the mode is in the band alone, the page header and the planning bar (`.mode-band`, the `--mode-*` tokens, set by `data-mode` on `.kalender`): olive for Plan, spruce green («Gran») for Bemanning, never the accent; the two lines that frame the band are darker than its fill in both palettes. An alert on the band is a solid chip with its own ink (`--alert-warn`, `--alert-block`, text `--alert-on`): never a tinted fill and never the ink of the band. Today is one line per pinned block (`.today-line`), not a mark per cell; it is seen wherever a day has content and breaks at the heading of a section, which lies over it (the planner's choice, where the rules have it unbroken).

Everything is stored in the browser (IndexedDB database `expo-planner`). There is no server.

## Conventions

- UI text is Norwegian (bokmål). Code, comments, commit messages and docs are English.
- The venue system is called **Venyou** (venue + you). "Venyoo" in `design_docs/` is a misspelling.
- Keep source and status apart: a Visma line always stays a Visma line; the planner toggles it in or out of the plan. Decisions the planner makes (Effekt, in plan, chosen work type, chosen location, hidden halls, the names of a project) are stored separately from imported data, keyed so they survive the next import.
- Visma is today's source of commercially ordered demand, not a part of the model. What the plan rests on is the chain from a quantity of a product type, through its KPI rate, to hours, and the product type's name is the key that joins an ordered line to its rate. Keep what is Visma's own (column names, the number and brackets around a type, that a stand number begins with the letter of its hall, the layout of the file) in the reader (`import/vismaExport.ts`: `vismaProductType`, `vismaHall`). The domain is handed a product type's name and a Hall/Sted text and knows nothing of how Visma writes them. The KPI table, the files the app writes and the planner's decisions are keyed by the product type's name (`productTypeKey` in `domain/kpi.ts`), so they hold when the orders come from another system.
- Reference data lives in editable tables in the app. A file import is at most an optional shortcut and must offer merge or replace when the table already has content.
- The planner tests from a clean state, so there are no migrations for data stored by earlier versions: when a stored shape changes, change it, and do not add code that reads the old one.
- Every mutation goes through `workspaceStore.tsx`, is undoable, and is persisted. Edits made in one user action form one undo step.
- New domain logic gets a test next to it. Tests ending in "(local data)" run against the real files in `example_data/` and are skipped when those are missing.

## Data and privacy

`example_data/` and all `.xlsx` files are git-ignored: they contain real customer and booking data. Never commit them, and do not copy customer names from them into tests, docs or commit messages. Event and project names already used in tests (VVS, Hage, Oslo Motor Show) are fine.

## Checking against the real files

With `example_data/` present, the local-data tests verify the readers of the Venyou, Visma and KPI files. To try the app in a browser without file dialogs, start the dev server and load a file through Vite's `/@fs/` path into the hidden file input (see the test notes in docs/TODO.md).

## Working with the user

- Work on a branch, push it, and report. The user says "merge to main"; then fast-forward main, run the tests, push, and delete the branch locally and on GitHub.
- The user tests in a clean state: Innstillinger → "Slett alt og start på nytt", then reads the sources in one by one.
- Staffing lines: the absence from Bemanning is in the Kalender (2026-10-07). Lines typed in by hand (Innleid, trade crews, Admin) came with the workbook only and are removed with it (2026-10-08); the Kalender's staffing lines are «Faste» and the two worked out from Bemanning. A replacement is deferred: the user will design a system for hired crew like Bemanning, covering the competence demand left after the permanent staff are assigned. Do not start on either until he asks.
