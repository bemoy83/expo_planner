# Kalender · Bemanning (Faste)

What the page does, as built. Moved from CLAUDE.md on 2026-10-23 so that it is read when work touches this part, not in every session; the rules of the code stay in CLAUDE.md.

The Kalender has two modes, switched first in the planning bar: **Plan**, the rows above, and **Bemanning**, who does the work: the permanent staff by name against the hours that remain per competence and day, over the whole period. In Bemanning the date header stays, the hall calendar becomes one line per project in view (`bemanning/ProjectLines.tsx`, `projectsInView.ts`), the staffing lines become the demand per competence, read from the planned FTE (`DemandRows.tsx`), which counts live while a stroke is drawn over the people: the days it covers and the «t igjen» of the label show what would remain after it (`shownBalance` in `BemanningScope.tsx`), and the rows become the people (`PeopleRows.tsx`). The switch zooms between the two column widths (`kalender/zoom.ts`, `useModeZoom.ts`). The width of a day is the style variable `--col-w`; lay out by it (`daysWide` in `kalender/layout.ts`), never by a width in pixels from `colW`, or the zoom has to draw every line again on each frame. A competence in focus opens a line under it for each project shown with hours of it in the days in view (`projectsWithWork`, which Innleid shares), what the project lacks once its tagged hours and its share of the rest are counted (`buildProjectBalance`), and the other competences shrink to their thin line; the line in focus is the brush, so a block painted on a project's line is tagged with the project (arrows up and down step between the lines, the sum line paints for none, and Shift stops where the project's demand is met). The project in focus has a line under it for each of its places, where it has two or more with hours of the competence in view (`placeLines`): a block painted there has the place too (`Assignment.hall`; the line «Prosjektets haller» paints «Ufordelt»), the project's own line paints for no place, which is then worked out (`workPlace.ts`), and Shift stops where the place's demand is met. A competence in focus is also the brush: it fills free time only, whole days or half, one person's hours open as a track of time per day with overtime (`UnfoldedPerson.tsx`, `TimeTrack.tsx`), and absence gives a person's hours back to the demand. The days in a row a person has one competence are drawn as one run with one name («Vegger · 5 d», `runs.ts`): a drawing only, the blocks are stored and edited per day as before, and another competence or an empty day ends a run, so a weekend does unless it is worked. The brush skips weekends and holidays when it is drawn across working days, and fills one from the start to the end of the normal day when it is picked by itself (`onlyOffDays`); such a day is drawn as a working day's, without hatch. Hatching on a person's day means absence: a neutral block where nothing is planned, and the planned block itself hatched in its colour where the absence cuts into it, with one badge, «Borte»: no reason for absence is stored, only there or away (2026-10-19). Absence for a part of a day takes only the time it covers out of a block (`workedParts`): those hours are back in the demand, the rest of the block counts, and only that stretch is hatched. A hatch says hours are not there: a person who is not available, hours of a block that do not count, and the hours the eraser would take. The rest of what a stroke would do is drawn flat: a tint where the brush would put time, a fainter tint on the day a move comes from, no edge on the blocks under the eraser, and one thin outline with a pill that sits on it. With any tool, an end of a run or of a single day is pulled along the days (`stretchRun` in `runs.ts`): outward the days get what the brush would give them drawn across, inward the run's blocks on them are removed, the day at the other end always stays, and the stroke keeps to that person's line. The width of a day is its normal day there, and the end stops where every two hours of it are worked (`stretchStops`: 09:00, 11:00, 13:30 and the end of the day with lunch from 11:30; counted in paid hours, so they hold wherever the lunch is laid): the last day is filled no further than the stop, and pulled inward the block the end lands in is cut to it. A brush stroke that is drawn out stops the same way on the day under the pointer, while a click fills its day. In columns too narrow to aim at two hours, both go by whole days. With Shift held while either is drawn out, the stroke goes no further than where the demand is met (`cellsToDemand` in `staffing.ts`): it ends on the last of its days that lacks hours of the competence, filled to the quarter of an hour they ask; Shift at the press of the brush is half days, as before. The lunch break is the planner's to lay, under Innstillinger → «Bemanning, normaltid og overhead» (`Settings.workday`). With Velg, too, a day's blocks are dragged to another day or person, and selected days are copied and pasted: a paste lands on the lines from the one under the pointer, the first person copied on that line and the next on the line under it. A click on a name opens the Person panel (`PersonPanel.tsx`): overtime per week against a limit, and absence entered as periods. What the parts of the mode share is held by `BemanningScope.tsx`, so the grid is not drawn again when the pointer moves over the people; it puts it together from hooks of its own (`useBemanningBalance`, `useBrush`, `usePeopleLines`, `useStrokePreview`, `useStaffingEdits`, `useStrokeInput`, `useBemanningKeys`), and gives the parts one object by the same names. Designed in `design_docs/bemanning-v2/`, on the rules of `design_docs/bemanning/RULES.md`.
